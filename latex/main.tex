\begin{abstract}
\noindent
We develop, in extended analytic and numerical detail, the geometric-phase
structure of bipartite photon-added coherent states (PACS). Starting from
the Fock-state definition of a single PACS, we re-derive step by step the
Berry phase of an individual branch, the Gram--Schmidt reduction of a
general nonorthogonal bipartite superposition to an effective two-qubit
pair, the reduced density matrices and their concurrence, the
branch-correlated PACS family generated by a local number rotation, the
global Berry phase of the entangled state (including, beyond the original
symmetric-balanced result, the general weighted case and an interference
term made manifest through a tunable branch phase), the Uhlmann
parallel-transport holonomy of the mixed reduced state, and the exact
nodal singularities of that holonomy. Every closed-form expression is
cross-checked against an independent numerical construction of the states
in a truncated Fock space --- bypassing the confluent hypergeometric
machinery entirely --- and against the two numerical tables published in
the original manuscript. This process both confirms the physical content
of the original results and corrects a transcription ambiguity in the
prefactor of the overlap and cross-number-operator formulas (the factor
$\Delta!$ belongs outside, not inside, the square root). We derive the
general (non-balanced) global Berry phase in closed form, obtain the exact
nodal coordinates $C_k$, $\mu_k$, $\alpha_k$ and the critical amplitude
$\alpha_g(m,n)$ by root-finding, and reproduce all seven figures and both
numerical tables of the original work with a fully commented, reproducible
Python/Jupyter implementation that saves every panel as a publication-ready
vector PDF.
This edition carries two corrections of substance. The first concerns the
Uhlmann connection itself: the sandwich expression
$\Lambda=\frac{1}{2i}S^{-1}[S,\dot S]S^{-1}$ used previously does not satisfy
the parallel-transport condition $[S,\dot S]=i\{\rho,\Lambda\}$, whose solution
carries a denominator $\lambda_j+\lambda_k$ rather than
$2\sqrt{\lambda_j\lambda_k}$. Correcting it leaves the closed form's functional
shape intact but replaces its parameter by
$b=\omega\sqrt{1+C^2-4p(1-p)}$, and thereby changes the nodal structure
qualitatively: instead of an infinite tower of singularities accumulating at
vanishing concurrence, each photon-addition separation supports exactly
$\Delta$ isolated nodes at $C_k=(2k+1)/2\Delta$, $k=0,\dots,\Delta-1$. Both the
corrected phase and the corrected nodes are confirmed against two independent
numerical constructions of the holonomy that use no connection formula at all
--- a discrete polar-decomposition algorithm and direct integration of the
transport equation --- which agree with each other and with the corrected form
to $10^{-6}$, and locate genuine zeros of the Uhlmann amplitude to
$10^{-15}$. The second correction concerns the second subsystem. Earlier
presentations dismissed $\rho_B$ as carrying zero geometric phase; we show that
this holds only for a strictly local cycle on $A$, as a consequence of
no-signalling, and that because $\rho_B$ descends from an \emph{entangled} PACS
it is isospectral to $\rho_A$, hence mixed with $\det\rho_B=(C/2)^2>0$, and
carries a genuine nonzero Uhlmann holonomy under its own cycle or under the
physically natural global two-mode evolution. We derive its winding number,
closed-form population and complete nodal structure, prove an exact
antisymmetry $\Phi_U^B=-\Phi_U^A$ for the crossed family, and show that
although the two subsystems share the same tower of nodal concurrences
$C_k=2\Delta/(2k+1)$, their nodal sets sit at different locations and obey
different existence criteria --- that of $A$ governed by $\alpha$, that of $B$
by $\beta$ --- so that one entangled state supports two geometrically distinct
singularity structures. Two further figures document this.
\end{abstract}

\section{Introduction}
% =========================================================================

Coherent states $\ket{\alpha}$ are the closest quantum analogue of a
classical oscillator: minimum-uncertainty Gaussian wave packets that remain
coherent under free evolution and provide the natural language of quantum
optics, from laser physics to continuous-variable quantum information.
Photon addition, $\ket{\alpha}\mapsto a^{\dagger n}\ket{\alpha}$ (suitably
renormalized), converts this classical-like state into a strongly
nonclassical one --- sub-Poissonian statistics, oscillatory
Mandel parameter, negative regions of the Wigner function --- while
preserving an essentially closed analytic structure controlled by a single
new discrete parameter, the photon-addition index $n$. This family, the
\emph{photon-added coherent states} (PACS), was introduced by Agarwal and
Tara \cite{AgarwalTara1991} and has since become a standard testbed for
exploring the boundary between classical and quantum light.

Two largely independent lines of research motivate the present work.

\paragraph{Entanglement of PACS.}
Dom\'inguez-Serna, Mendieta-Jim\'enez, and Rojas \cite{DominguezSerna2016}
studied entanglement in bipartite superpositions of PACS branches built
from \emph{nonorthogonal} coherent-state kets. Because
$\braket{\alpha,m}{\beta,n}\neq0$ in general, such superpositions cannot be
treated as ordinary two-qubit states without first performing an explicit
orthogonalization; once this is done (Gram--Schmidt reduction, reviewed in
Sec.~\ref{sec:general-bipartite} below), the reduced density matrices
acquire a rank-2 support and an effective two-qubit concurrence can be
computed via Wootters' formula \cite{Wootters1998}. The result is a family
in which coherent amplitudes, discrete photon-addition indices, and
entanglement coexist and can be tuned largely independently --- a rich
playground that the present work exploits directly.

\paragraph{Geometric phases of coherent-state superpositions.}
Separately, the geometric (Berry) phase \cite{Berry1984} accumulated by
cyclically evolved coherent-state superpositions has been studied by several
groups. Mohammadi Almas, Najarbashi, and Tavana
\cite{MohammadiAlmas2022} obtained the cyclic geometric phase of
two-mode entangled coherent states and emphasized how branch weights,
wavefunction overlaps, and interference terms enter the result. Yang
\emph{et al.} \cite{Yang2011} extended geometric-phase considerations to
nonlinear coherent and squeezed states, showing that nonlinearity of the
oscillator algebra is imprinted directly on the phase. Neither line of work,
however, addressed \emph{photon-added} coherent states specifically.

\paragraph{Mixed-state holonomy.}
For a genuinely mixed quantum state --- such as the reduced density matrix
of an entangled bipartite pure state --- the ordinary Berry-phase
construction does not directly apply, because there is no single global
phase to track. Uhlmann's parallel-transport construction
\cite{Uhlmann1986} resolves this by lifting $\rho$ to a purification
amplitude $W$ with $\rho=WW^\dagger$ and defining a connection on the space
of such amplitudes; the resulting holonomy is a genuine geometric
invariant of the mixed-state path, reducing to the ordinary Berry phase in
the pure-state limit. Wang \emph{et al.} \cite{Wang2023} recently
developed the analytic Uhlmann--Berry correspondence for both bosonic and
fermionic coherent states, establishing coherent states as a particularly
transparent laboratory for this correspondence because their algebra keeps
every step in closed form.

\paragraph{The gap addressed here.}
Entanglement of PACS, geometric phases of entangled coherent states,
geometric phases of \emph{generalized} (photon-added) coherent states, and
Uhlmann phases of \emph{ordinary} coherent states have thus all been
studied --- but never jointly. The present work fills exactly this gap: we
compute the global Berry phase of an \emph{entangled bipartite PACS} under
a local photon-number rotation, and the Uhlmann holonomy of its
\emph{mixed reduced state}, in a single unified analytic and numerical
framework. Photon addition introduces the discrete separation
\begin{equation}
\Delta \;=\; n-m ,
\label{eq:Delta-def}
\end{equation}
which turns out to act as an effective \emph{winding number} for the
reduced-state isospectral loop (Sec.~\ref{sec:local-cycle}): it multiplies
the argument of the trigonometric functions that build the Uhlmann
holonomy (Sec.~\ref{sec:uhlmann}), and it is the parameter that indexes the
tower of exact nodal singularities derived in
Sec.~\ref{sec:nodal}. The main results of this extended treatment are:

\begin{enumerate}[label=(\roman*)]
\item a fully re-derived, step-by-step account of the single-PACS Berry
      phase, the Gram--Schmidt bipartite reduction, and the resulting
      concurrence (Secs.~\ref{sec:pacs}--\ref{sec:general-bipartite});
\item the closed-form global Berry phase of the branch-correlated
      entangled PACS, generalized here to \emph{arbitrary} branch weight
      $\mu$ (the original manuscript treats only the balanced case
      $\mu=\nu=1/\sqrt2$), together with the branch-phase-controlled
      interference fringes (Sec.~\ref{sec:global-berry});
\item the closed-form Uhlmann phase of the mixed reduced state and its
      exact nodal singularities, including analytic nodal coordinates
      $(C_k,\mu_k,\alpha_k)$ and the critical amplitude $\alpha_g(m,n)$ that
      bounds them (Secs.~\ref{sec:uhlmann}--\ref{sec:nodal});
\item a correction to the Uhlmann connection itself: the sandwich form
      $\Lambda=\frac{1}{2i}S^{-1}[S,\dot S]S^{-1}$ does not solve the
      parallel-transport condition $[S,\dot S]=i\{\rho,\Lambda\}$, and
      replacing it changes the parameter $b$ and turns the nodal set from an
      infinite accumulating tower into exactly $\Delta$ isolated
      singularities at $C_k=(2k+1)/2\Delta$; this is verified against two
      independent numerical constructions of the holonomy
      (Secs.~\ref{sec:uhlmann}, \ref{sec:nodal}, \ref{sec:numerics});
\item a full treatment of the \emph{second} subsystem, correcting the earlier
      claim that $\rho_B$ carries no geometric phase: we establish that this is
      an artifact of restricting attention to a strictly local cycle on $A$,
      derive $\omega_B$, $p_B$ and the closed-form $\Phi_U^B$, prove the exact
      antisymmetry $\Phi_U^B=-\Phi_U^A$ of the crossed family, and obtain the
      complete nodal structure of $B$ --- same tower $C_k$, different locations,
      and an existence criterion governed by $\beta$ rather than $\alpha$
      (Secs.~\ref{sec:subsystemB}--\ref{sec:antisymmetry} and
      Sec.~\ref{sec:nodalB});
\item an explicit, independent \emph{numerical validation layer}: every
      closed-form expression is checked against a brute-force
      truncated-Fock-space construction of the same physical quantity, with
      no recourse to confluent hypergeometric identities
      (Sec.~\ref{sec:numerics}); this uncovers and corrects a
      typographical ambiguity in the prefactor of Eqs.~(27) and (37) of the
      original manuscript;
\item a fully commented, freely reproducible Python/Jupyter notebook that
      regenerates every figure and table in this paper and exports them as
      publication-ready vector PDFs (Sec.~\ref{sec:numerics} and the
      accompanying supplementary code).
\end{enumerate}

\section{Photon-added coherent states}
\label{sec:pacs}
% =========================================================================

\subsection{Definition and Fock expansion}

A photon-added coherent state (PACS) is obtained by applying the creation
operator $n$ times to a Glauber coherent state and renormalizing,
\begin{equation}
\ket{\alpha,n} \;=\; \frac{a^{\dagger n}\ket{\alpha}}
{\big[\bra{\alpha}a^{n}a^{\dagger n}\ket{\alpha}\big]^{1/2}} .
\label{eq:pacs-def}
\end{equation}
To make the normalization and every later overlap fully explicit, we carry
out the Fock expansion in more detail than the original manuscript. Writing
$\ket{\alpha}=e^{-|\alpha|^2/2}\sum_{j\ge0}\alpha^j/\sqrt{j!}\,\ket{j}$ and
using
$a^{\dagger n}\ket{j}=\sqrt{(j+1)(j+2)\cdots(j+n)}\,\ket{j+n}=\sqrt{(j+n)!/j!}\,\ket{j+n}$,
\begin{equation}
a^{\dagger n}\ket{\alpha}
= e^{-|\alpha|^2/2}\sum_{j=0}^{\infty}\frac{\alpha^{j}}{\sqrt{j!}}
\sqrt{\frac{(j+n)!}{j!}}\;\ket{j+n}
= e^{-|\alpha|^2/2}\sum_{k=0}^{\infty}\frac{\alpha^{k}\sqrt{(n+k)!}}{k!}\;\ket{n+k},
\label{eq:adag-alpha}
\end{equation}
after renaming $k\equiv j$. The normalization constant is fixed by the
standard PACS identity
$\bra{\alpha}a^{n}a^{\dagger n}\ket{\alpha}=n!\,L_n(-|\alpha|^2)$, with
$L_n$ the (ordinary, $\alpha=0$) Laguerre polynomial,
\begin{equation}
L_m(x)=\sum_{r=0}^{m}\frac{(-1)^r x^r m!}{(r!)^2(m-r)!}.
\label{eq:laguerre-def}
\end{equation}
Combining Eqs.~\eqref{eq:adag-alpha}--\eqref{eq:laguerre-def} with
Eq.~\eqref{eq:pacs-def} gives the normalized Fock expansion
\begin{equation}
\ket{\alpha,n} = \frac{e^{-|\alpha|^2/2}}{\sqrt{n!\,L_n(-|\alpha|^2)}}
\sum_{k=0}^{\infty}\frac{\alpha^{k}\sqrt{(n+k)!}}{k!}\;\ket{n+k}.
\label{eq:pacs-fock}
\end{equation}
This is the working definition used throughout the paper and throughout the
accompanying notebook, where it is implemented directly (function
\texttt{pacs\_fock\_amplitudes}) and used as an independent numerical
ground truth against which every closed-form expression below is checked.

\subsection{Berry phase of a single PACS}

Consider the number-rotation unitary $U(\phi)=e^{-ia^\dagger a\,\phi}$. The
Berry connection generated by cycling $\ket{\alpha,n}$ under $U(\phi)$ is
\begin{equation}
\mathcal A_\phi = i\bra{\alpha,n}U^\dagger(\phi)\,\partial_\phi U(\phi)\ket{\alpha,n}.
\label{eq:connection-def}
\end{equation}
Since the number operator commutes with any function of itself,
$U^\dagger(\phi)\partial_\phi U(\phi)=U^\dagger(\phi)(-ia^\dagger a)U(\phi)=-ia^\dagger a$,
so that
\begin{equation}
\mathcal A_\phi = \bra{\alpha,n}a^\dagger a\ket{\alpha,n} \equiv \bar n_{\alpha,n},
\label{eq:connection-nbar}
\end{equation}
independent of $\phi$: the PACS is an \emph{instantaneous eigenstate} of the
generator under this particular evolution, so its expectation value of
$a^\dagger a$ does not change along the cycle. The mean photon number
$\bar n_{\alpha,n}$ follows from the three-term (Laguerre) recursion applied
to Eq.~\eqref{eq:pacs-fock},
\begin{equation}
\bar n_{\alpha,n} = (n+1)\,\frac{L_{n+1}(-|\alpha|^2)}{L_n(-|\alpha|^2)} - 1,
\label{eq:nbar}
\end{equation}
and, because the connection is constant, the cyclic Berry phase is simply
\begin{equation}
\Phi_B^{(n)} = \int_0^{2\pi}\mathcal A_\phi\,d\phi = 2\pi\,\bar n_{\alpha,n}.
\label{eq:berry-single}
\end{equation}
As a limiting check, $n=0$ reduces Eq.~\eqref{eq:pacs-fock} to the ordinary
coherent state, and Eq.~\eqref{eq:nbar} correctly gives
$\bar n_{\alpha,0}=|\alpha|^2$ (verified numerically in the notebook,
Sec.~\ref{sec:numerics}).

\section{General bipartite PACS model and logical-qubit reduction}
\label{sec:general-bipartite}
% =========================================================================

The generic bipartite superposition considered in this framework mixes two
\emph{nonorthogonal} PACS branches on each side,
\begin{equation}
\ket{\Phi} = \frac{\mu}{\mathcal N}\ket{\alpha,m}\ket{\delta,d}
+ \frac{\nu}{\mathcal N}\ket{\gamma,l}\ket{\beta,n},
\label{eq:general-bipartite}
\end{equation}
with normalization
\begin{equation}
\mathcal N^2 = |\mu|^2+|\nu|^2
+\mu^*\nu\,\braket{\alpha,m}{\gamma,l}\braket{\delta,d}{\beta,n}
+\mu\nu^*\,\braket{\gamma,l}{\alpha,m}\braket{\beta,n}{\delta,d}.
\label{eq:general-norm}
\end{equation}
Because $\braket{\alpha,m}{\gamma,l}$ and $\braket{\delta,d}{\beta,n}$ are
generally nonzero, $\{\ket{\alpha,m},\ket{\gamma,l}\}$ and
$\{\ket{\beta,n},\ket{\delta,d}\}$ are \emph{not} orthonormal bases, so
Eq.~\eqref{eq:general-bipartite} is not directly a two-qubit state. The
standard remedy is Gram--Schmidt orthogonalization on each side,
\begin{equation}
\ket{0_A}=\ket{\alpha,m},\qquad
\ket{1_A}=\frac{\ket{\gamma,l}-p_1\ket{\alpha,m}}{N_1},\qquad
\ket{0_B}=\ket{\beta,n},\qquad
\ket{1_B}=\frac{\ket{\delta,d}-p_2\ket{\beta,n}}{N_2},
\label{eq:gram-schmidt}
\end{equation}
with
\begin{equation}
p_1=\braket{\alpha,m}{\gamma,l},\qquad p_2=\braket{\beta,n}{\delta,d},\qquad
N_j=\sqrt{1-|p_j|^2}.
\label{eq:gs-coeffs}
\end{equation}
Substituting Eq.~\eqref{eq:gram-schmidt} into Eq.~\eqref{eq:general-bipartite}
converts the nonorthogonal superposition into a genuine, normalized,
three-term two-qubit state,
\begin{equation}
\ket{\Phi} = M\ket{0_A0_B} + \frac{\mu N_2}{\mathcal N}\ket{0_A1_B}
+ \frac{\nu N_1}{\mathcal N}\ket{1_A0_B},
\qquad
M=\frac{\mu p_2+\nu p_1}{\mathcal N}.
\label{eq:logical-state}
\end{equation}
Tracing out either subsystem gives rank-2 reduced density matrices with
identical nonzero spectrum
\begin{equation}
\lambda_\pm = \frac12 \pm \frac12\sqrt{1-4\left(\frac{\mu\nu N_1N_2}{\mathcal N^2}\right)^2},
\label{eq:lambda-pm}
\end{equation}
and Wootters' concurrence formula for a pure state with effective
two-qubit support, $C=\sqrt{2(1-\Tr\rho_s^2)}=2\sqrt{\det\rho_s}$
($s=A,B$) \cite{Wootters1998}, evaluates to
\begin{equation}
C = 2\,\frac{\mu\nu N_1N_2}{\mathcal N^2}.
\label{eq:concurrence-general}
\end{equation}
For the ``crossed'' choice $\gamma=\beta$, $l=m$, $\delta=\alpha$, $d=n$
(which pairs each amplitude with itself across subsystems, the case used
throughout the rest of this paper), this reduces to
\begin{equation}
C = \frac{2|\mu\nu|}{\mathcal N^2}\sqrt{\big(1-|\braket{\alpha,m}{\beta,m}|^2\big)\big(1-|\braket{\alpha,n}{\beta,n}|^2\big)}.
\label{eq:concurrence-crossed}
\end{equation}

\section{Local cyclic rotation and the branch-correlated PACS family}
\label{sec:local-cycle}
% =========================================================================

A convenient crossed family retains a single coherent amplitude per
subsystem while pairing photon-addition indices across the two branches,
\begin{equation}
\ket{\psi} = \frac{\mu}{\mathcal N}\ket{\alpha,m}\ket{\beta,n}
+\frac{\nu}{\mathcal N}\ket{\beta,n}\ket{\alpha,m}.
\label{eq:swap-family}
\end{equation}
Under the local evolution $U_A(\phi)=e^{-ia_A^\dagger a_A\phi}\otimes I_B$,
the coherent amplitudes rotate as $\alpha\mapsto\alpha e^{-i\phi}$,
$\beta\mapsto\beta e^{-i\phi}$, while the photon-addition indices contribute
phases $e^{-im\phi}$, $e^{-in\phi}$. In the logical (Gram--Schmidt) basis
the reduced state follows an \emph{isospectral} orbit
\begin{equation}
\rho_A(\phi) = V(\phi)\rho_A(0)V^\dagger(\phi),\qquad
V(\phi)=e^{i\frac{\omega}{2}\sigma_z\phi},\qquad
\omega = n-m \in \mathbb Z,
\label{eq:isospectral-orbit}
\end{equation}
so that the eigenvalues of $\rho_A(\phi)$ never change --- only the
relative phase between the two logical basis states precesses. Because
$V(2\pi)=(-1)^\omega I$, the density-matrix loop is exactly cyclic
regardless of the parity of $\omega$.

For the remainder of this paper we specialize to the \emph{branch-correlated}
state
\begin{equation}
\ket{\Phi} = \frac{1}{\mathcal N}\Big[\mu\,\ket{\alpha,m}_A\ket{\beta,m}_B
+\nu\,\ket{\alpha,n}_A\ket{\beta,n}_B\Big],
\label{eq:branch-correlated}
\end{equation}
which keeps a common coherent amplitude within each subsystem while
retaining the discrete pair $(m,n)$ that generates $\omega=n-m$. For
$n\ge m$ real $\alpha$, define
\begin{equation}
g_\alpha \equiv \braket{\alpha,m}{\alpha,n}
= \frac{e^{-|\alpha|^2}(\alpha^*)^{n-m}n!}{(n-m)!\sqrt{n!\,m!\,L_n(-|\alpha|^2)L_m(-|\alpha|^2)}}
\;\hyp{n+1}{n-m+1}{|\alpha|^2},
\label{eq:g-overlap-corrected}
\end{equation}
and $h_\beta$ by the same expression with $\alpha\to\beta$. The full,
explicit derivation of Eq.~\eqref{eq:g-overlap-corrected} --- together with
a discussion of a prefactor ambiguity present in the original
manuscript's Eq.~(27), which places the factor $(n-m)!$ \emph{inside}
rather than outside the square root --- is given in
Appendix~\ref{app:overlaps}. With
Eq.~\eqref{eq:g-overlap-corrected},
\begin{equation}
N_1=\sqrt{1-|g_\alpha|^2},\qquad N_2=\sqrt{1-|h_\beta|^2},\qquad
\mathcal N^2 = |\mu|^2+|\nu|^2+2\,\mathrm{Re}(\mu^*\nu\,g_\alpha h_\beta),
\label{eq:branch-normalization}
\end{equation}
\begin{equation}
\det\rho_A = \frac{|\mu\nu|^2(1-|g_\alpha|^2)(1-|h_\beta|^2)}{\mathcal N^4}
= \left(\frac{C}{2}\right)^2 ,
\label{eq:detA-branch}
\end{equation}
and, in the natural Gram--Schmidt basis, the reduced population of
subsystem $A$ is
\begin{equation}
p_A \equiv (\rho_A)_{22} = \frac{|\nu|^2(1-|g_\alpha|^2)}{\mathcal N^2},
\label{eq:pA-def}
\end{equation}
with the analogous $p_B$ obtained by $g_\alpha\to h_\beta$ if subsystem $B$
is independently cycled.

\section{Global Berry phase of the entangled PACS}
\label{sec:global-berry}
% =========================================================================

\subsection{General weighted formula (extension beyond the balanced case)}

Under the local cycle $U_A(\phi)$, the Berry connection of the
\emph{global} pure state $\ket{\Phi}$ is
\begin{equation}
\mathcal A_\phi = \bra{\Phi}(a_A^\dagger a_A\otimes I_B)\ket{\Phi}.
\label{eq:global-connection}
\end{equation}
The original manuscript evaluates this only for the balanced weight
$\mu=\nu=1/\sqrt2$. Here we carry out the substitution of
Eq.~\eqref{eq:branch-correlated} into Eq.~\eqref{eq:global-connection} for
\emph{arbitrary} real $\mu\in[0,1]$, $\nu=\sqrt{1-\mu^2}$, and real
$\alpha=\beta$ (so that $h_\beta=g_\alpha\equiv g$). Expanding the four
cross terms of the bra--ket product and using
$\braket{\alpha,m}{\alpha,m}=\braket{\alpha,n}{\alpha,n}=1$,
$\braket{\alpha,m}{\alpha,n}=g$,
\begin{equation}
\mathcal A_\phi = \frac{1}{\mathcal N^2}\Big[\mu^2\bar n_{\alpha,m}
+\nu^2\bar n_{\alpha,n} + 2\mu\nu\,\mathrm{Re}\big(\kappa_\alpha^{mn}\,g\big)\Big],
\qquad
\mathcal N^2 = 1+2\mu\nu g^2,
\label{eq:general-connection}
\end{equation}
where $\kappa_\alpha^{mn}\equiv\bra{\alpha,m}a^\dagger a\ket{\alpha,n}$ is
the cross number-operator matrix element derived in
Appendix~\ref{app:cross-element}. Since $\mathcal A_\phi$ is again
independent of $\phi$, $\Phi_B^{AB}=2\pi\mathcal A_\phi\;(\mathrm{mod}\ 2\pi)$.
Introducing the tunable relative branch phase of Eq.~\eqref{eq:branch-phase}
below (which amounts to $\nu\to\nu e^{i\chi}$ in the cross term), the
\emph{fully general} closed form is
\begin{equation}
\boxed{\;
\frac{\Phi_B^{AB}(\mu,\chi)}{2\pi} =
\frac{\mu^2\bar n_{\alpha,m}+\nu^2\bar n_{\alpha,n}+2\mu\nu\, g^2 Q_{mn}(\alpha^2)\cos\chi}
{1+2\mu\nu\,g^2\cos\chi}
\;}
\label{eq:general-berry-formula}
\end{equation}
where $Q_{mn}$ is defined in Eq.~\eqref{eq:Qmn-def} below and we used
$\kappa_\alpha^{mn}=g\,Q_{mn}(\alpha^2)$
(Appendix~\ref{app:cross-element}). Equation~\eqref{eq:general-berry-formula}
is the formula used to produce Fig.~\ref{fig:fig4} (a genuine
$(\mu,\alpha)$ phase map) and reduces, at $\mu=\nu=1/\sqrt2$ and $\chi=0$,
to the balanced-case result of the original manuscript, recovered next.

\subsection{Balanced symmetric case and explicit concurrence dependence}

Setting $\beta=\alpha\in\mathbb R$ and $\mu=\nu=1/\sqrt2$ in
Eq.~\eqref{eq:general-berry-formula} ($2\mu\nu=1$, $\chi=0$),
\begin{equation}
\frac{\Phi_B^{AB}}{2\pi} = \frac{\bar n_{\rm av} + g^2 Q_{mn}}{1+g^2},
\qquad
\bar n_{\rm av}\equiv\frac{\bar n_{\alpha,m}+\bar n_{\alpha,n}}{2}.
\label{eq:berry-balanced}
\end{equation}
Because the concurrence in this case is $C=(1-g^2)/(1+g^2)$, i.e.
$g^2=(1-C)/(1+C)$, Eq.~\eqref{eq:berry-balanced} can be rewritten as an
\emph{affine function of the concurrence} at fixed $m,n,\alpha$,
\begin{equation}
\frac{\Phi_B^{AB}}{2\pi} = \frac{1+C}{2}\,\bar n_{\rm av} + \frac{1-C}{2}\,Q_{mn}.
\label{eq:berry-vs-C}
\end{equation}
This is one of the cleanest statements in the whole framework: for a
\emph{pure} entangled state, the global geometric phase interpolates
linearly between the mean-photon-number average $\bar n_{\rm av}$ (at
$C=0$, a separable-limit-like weighting) and the interference term
$Q_{mn}$ (at $C=1$, maximal concurrence).

\subsection{Making the interference oscillatory: branch phase $\chi$}

The local cycle in $\phi$ produces a connection that already contains a
coherent cross term, $g^2Q_{mn}$, yet the resulting phase
Eq.~\eqref{eq:berry-balanced} shows no oscillation in $\phi$ --- the
interference is present but ``hidden'' inside a $\phi$-independent
constant. To expose it explicitly, introduce a controllable relative phase
between the two branches,
\begin{equation}
\ket{\Phi(\chi)} \propto \ket{\alpha,m}\ket{\alpha,m}
+ e^{i\chi}\ket{\alpha,n}\ket{\alpha,n},
\label{eq:branch-phase}
\end{equation}
for which the balanced symmetric result becomes
\begin{equation}
\frac{\Phi_B(\chi)}{2\pi} = \frac{\bar n_{\rm av}+g^2Q_{mn}\cos\chi}{1+g^2\cos\chi},
\label{eq:berry-chi}
\end{equation}
manifestly $2\pi$-periodic and oscillatory in $\chi$, unlike the original
$\phi$-cycle. Figure~\ref{fig:fig3} shows this interferometric signature
directly.

\section{Uhlmann phase of the reduced state}
\label{sec:uhlmann}
% =========================================================================

\subsection{Parallel transport and closed form}

For a mixed state $\rho=WW^\dagger$ with amplitude $W=\sqrt\rho\,U$ and $U$
unitary, Uhlmann's parallel-transport condition is that $W^\dagger\dot W$ be
Hermitian. Writing $S=\sqrt\rho$ and $\dot U=-i\Lambda U$,
\begin{equation}
W^\dagger\dot W = U^\dagger\big(S\dot S - i\rho\Lambda\big)U ,
\end{equation}
and imposing Hermiticity gives the defining equation of the Uhlmann connection,
\begin{equation}
\boxed{\;[S,\dot S] \;=\; i\,\{\rho,\Lambda\}\;}
\label{eq:uhlmann-ode}
\end{equation}
an \emph{anticommutator} (Sylvester) equation for $\Lambda$. In the eigenbasis
of $\rho$ (eigenvalues $\lambda_j$) it is solved elementwise by
\begin{equation}
\Lambda_{jk} \;=\; \frac{-i\,[S,\dot S]_{jk}}{\lambda_j+\lambda_k}\, .
\label{eq:Lambda-correct}
\end{equation}

\paragraph{A correction to the earlier closed form.}
The underlying manuscript instead writes
$\Lambda=\frac{1}{2i}S^{-1}[S,\dot S]S^{-1}$, whose eigenbasis elements carry a
denominator $2\sqrt{\lambda_j\lambda_k}$ rather than $\lambda_j+\lambda_k$. The
two agree only when $\lambda_j=\lambda_k$, i.e.\ for the maximally mixed state;
away from that point the sandwich form does \emph{not} solve
Eq.~\eqref{eq:uhlmann-ode} and is therefore not the Uhlmann connection. We
verify this in Sec.~\ref{sec:numerics} against two independent numerical
constructions of the holonomy that use no connection formula at all. The final
closed form retains exactly the same functional shape, but with a different
parameter $b$; the nodal analysis of Sec.~\ref{sec:nodal} changes
qualitatively as a result.

For the isospectral loop of Eq.~\eqref{eq:isospectral-orbit},
$\dot S = i[G,S]$ with generator
\begin{equation}
G = \frac{\omega}{2}\sigma_z .
\label{eq:G-def}
\end{equation}
Then $[S,\dot S]_{jk}=-iG_{jk}(\sqrt{\lambda_j}-\sqrt{\lambda_k})^2$, so
Eq.~\eqref{eq:Lambda-correct} gives a purely off-diagonal connection
\begin{equation}
\Lambda_{jk} = -\,G_{jk}\,\frac{(\sqrt{\lambda_j}-\sqrt{\lambda_k})^2}{\lambda_j+\lambda_k},
\qquad j\neq k .
\label{eq:Lambda-explicit}
\end{equation}
Because $\Tr\rho=1$ we have $\lambda_j+\lambda_k=1$ for $j\neq k$, and
$2\sqrt{\lambda_+\lambda_-}=2\sqrt{\det\rho}=C$, so
$(\sqrt{\lambda_+}-\sqrt{\lambda_-})^2=1-C$ and the connection collapses to the
remarkably simple statement
\begin{equation}
\Lambda_{\rm od} = -(1-C)\,G_{\rm od}
\qquad\Longrightarrow\qquad
(G+\Lambda)_{\rm od} = C\,G_{\rm od} :
\label{eq:GplusLambda}
\end{equation}
\emph{the concurrence is exactly the factor by which parallel transport
renormalizes the off-diagonal part of the generator.} Solving the resulting
constant-coefficient equation over one period gives the holonomy operator
\begin{equation}
U_{\rm hol}(2\pi) = e^{iG2\pi}\,e^{-i(G+\widetilde\Lambda)2\pi},
\label{eq:holonomy-op}
\end{equation}
and the Uhlmann phase is read off from
\begin{equation}
\Phi_U = \Arg\Tr\big[\rho_A(0)\,U_{\rm hol}(2\pi)\big].
\label{eq:phiU-def}
\end{equation}
Writing
\begin{equation}
p\equiv(\rho_A)_{22},\qquad r=\sqrt{1-4\det\rho_A},
\label{eq:p-r-def}
\end{equation}
the Bloch vector of $\rho_A(0)$ has $n_z=1-2p$, $n_x=2\sqrt{p(1-p)-\det\rho_A}$
and $|\vec n|=r$. Decomposing $G$ into components parallel and perpendicular to
$\vec n$ and applying Eq.~\eqref{eq:GplusLambda} to the perpendicular part only,
$G+\widetilde\Lambda=\frac12\vec h\cdot\vec\tau$ with
$h_\parallel=\omega\cos\theta$, $h_\perp=C\,\omega\sin\theta$ and
$\cos\theta=(1-2p)/r$. Since $e^{iG2\pi}=(-1)^\omega\mathbb 1$ for integer
$\omega$, carrying out the matrix exponentials in Eq.~\eqref{eq:holonomy-op}
gives
\begin{equation}
\Phi_U = \Arg\left[(-1)^\omega\cos(\pi b) - i\,\frac{\omega(1-2p)}{b}\sin(\pi b)\right],
\qquad
\boxed{\;b = \omega\sqrt{1+C^2-4p(1-p)}\;}
\label{eq:phiU-closed}
\end{equation}
where the compact form of $b$ follows from
$b^2=\omega^2[\cos^2\theta+C^2\sin^2\theta]$ after substituting $r^2=1-C^2$ and
$\det\rho_A=C^2/4$. The functional form is identical to that of the underlying
manuscript; the parameter differs, that manuscript having
$b=\omega\sqrt{p(1-p)/\det\rho_A}$ as a consequence of
Eq.~\eqref{eq:Lambda-correct} being replaced by the sandwich form. Two limits
check Eq.~\eqref{eq:phiU-closed}: if $G$ commutes with $\rho_A$ (so $\sin\theta=0$,
$p\to0,1$) the state does not move and $\Phi_U=0$; and for the maximally mixed
state the loop is again trivial. The Uhlmann phase is controlled \emph{jointly}
by the entanglement (through $C$) and by the local population $p$ ---
unlike the pure-state Berry phase of Eq.~\eqref{eq:berry-vs-C}, which in
the balanced case depends on the concurrence \emph{alone}. The expression
assumes a full-rank $\rho_A$; the rank-deficient limit $\det\rho_A\to0$
must be taken separately and is discussed in Sec.~\ref{sec:numerics}.

A useful special case, used throughout Sec.~\ref{sec:nodal}, is the
population-symmetric point:
\begin{equation}
p=\tfrac12
\qquad\Longrightarrow\qquad
b=\omega\,C ,
\label{eq:b-at-half}
\end{equation}
so that at $p=1/2$ the parameter $b$ is \emph{proportional} to the concurrence
(the earlier form gave the reciprocal, $b=\omega/C$).

\subsection{Subsystem $B$ also carries a nonzero Uhlmann holonomy}
\label{sec:subsystemB}

Earlier presentations of this material disposed of subsystem $B$ in a single
sentence: a local unitary on $A$ leaves $\rho_B$ invariant, hence $B$ ``traces
a trivial path and carries zero geometric phase''. That sentence is true only
of one specific cycle, and read on its own it is seriously misleading --- it
suggests that $\rho_B$ has no Uhlmann geometry at all. It does. Two statements
must be kept apart.

\paragraph{(i) Under a strictly local cycle $U_A(\phi)\otimes I_B$, $\rho_B$ is
exactly constant.}
This is not an artifact of the model but a theorem: for any $\rho_{AB}$ and any
local unitary $U_A$, cyclicity of the partial trace gives
\begin{equation}
\Tr_A\big[(U_A\otimes I)\rho_{AB}(U_A^\dagger\otimes I)\big]
=\sum_{a,a_1,a_2}\!\langle a|U_A|a_1\rangle\langle a_2|U_A^\dagger|a\rangle\,
\langle a_1|\rho_{AB}|a_2\rangle
=\Tr_A[\rho_{AB}] .
\label{eq:no-signalling}
\end{equation}
Equation~\eqref{eq:no-signalling} is the content of \emph{no-signalling}: if
cycling $\phi$ on $A$ could alter $\rho_B$, then $A$ could transmit information
to $B$ instantaneously. We confirm it to machine precision in
Sec.~\ref{sec:numerics} ($\max_\phi|\Delta(\rho_B)_{01}|<3\times10^{-16}$).
In this restricted sense --- and only in this sense --- $\Phi_U^B=0$.

\paragraph{(ii) Nevertheless $\rho_B$ possesses its own, generally nonzero,
Uhlmann phase.}
The reason is precisely that $\rho_B$ descends from an \emph{entangled} PACS:
for a bipartite pure state the Schmidt decomposition makes $\rho_A$ and
$\rho_B$ \emph{isospectral}, so
\begin{equation}
\det\rho_B=\det\rho_A=\Big(\frac{C}{2}\Big)^{2}>0 .
\label{eq:isospectral-det}
\end{equation}
$\rho_B$ is therefore a full-rank mixed state on its logical support, and
\emph{the very same concurrence governs the geometry of both sides}. What was
missing was not the geometry but the cycle that activates it. Two such cycles
are physically available: the independent cycle $I_A\otimes U_B(\phi)$, and ---
more importantly --- the \emph{global} cycle
\begin{equation}
U_{AB}(\phi)=U_A(\phi)\otimes U_B(\phi)=e^{-i(a_A^\dagger a_A+a_B^\dagger a_B)\phi},
\label{eq:global-cycle}
\end{equation}
which is nothing but the free evolution of a propagating two-mode field, and is
thus the physically natural cycle for an entangled PACS. Since
$\rho_A(\phi)=U_A\rho_A(0)U_A^\dagger$ and $\rho_B(\phi)=U_B\rho_B(0)U_B^\dagger$
under Eq.~\eqref{eq:global-cycle}, \emph{both} reduced states traverse
isospectral loops simultaneously and \emph{both} holonomies are nonzero.

\paragraph{(iii) The winding number of $B$ need not equal that of $A$.}
Reading off the index phases $e^{-im\phi}$, $e^{-in\phi}$ that remain explicit
in each branch --- exactly as in Eq.~(18) of the underlying PACS
manuscript --- gives the winding number of each side directly:
\begin{center}
\begin{tabular}{lccc}
\toprule
family & branches & $\omega_A$ & $\omega_B$\\
\midrule
branch-correlated, Eq.~\eqref{eq:branch-correlated}
& $\ket{\alpha,m}\ket{\beta,m}\,,\;\ket{\alpha,n}\ket{\beta,n}$
& $+\Delta$ & $+\Delta$\\
crossed (swap), Eq.~\eqref{eq:swap-family}
& $\ket{\alpha,m}\ket{\beta,n}\,,\;\ket{\beta,n}\ket{\alpha,m}$
& $+\Delta$ & $\boldsymbol{-\Delta}$\\
\bottomrule
\end{tabular}
\end{center}
In the crossed family the photon-addition indices are \emph{interchanged}
between $A$ and $B$, so the two subsystems wind in \emph{opposite senses}.

\subsection{Closed forms for subsystem $B$}
\label{sec:B-closed}

Building the Gram--Schmidt basis on each side and noting that the branch of
weight $\nu$ supplies the state $\ket{1}$ on \emph{both} sides, one obtains for
the branch-correlated family with generally unequal amplitudes
($q_\alpha\equiv|g_{mn}(\alpha)|^2$, $q_\beta\equiv|h_{mn}(\beta)|^2$)
\begin{equation}
\mathcal N^2=1+2\mu\nu\,g_{mn}(\alpha)h_{mn}(\beta),\qquad
p_A=\frac{\nu^2(1-q_\alpha)}{\mathcal N^2},\qquad
p_B=\frac{\nu^2(1-q_\beta)}{\mathcal N^2},
\label{eq:pB-correlated}
\end{equation}
\begin{equation}
\det\rho_A=\det\rho_B=\frac{\mu^2\nu^2(1-q_\alpha)(1-q_\beta)}{\mathcal N^4}
=\Big(\frac{C}{2}\Big)^2,
\qquad \omega_A=\omega_B=+\Delta .
\label{eq:detB-correlated}
\end{equation}
Hence $p_A=p_B$ \emph{if and only if} $q_\alpha=q_\beta$, i.e.\ (for real
amplitudes) $\beta=\alpha$. Away from the symmetric point the two Uhlmann
phases are genuinely different even though they share a concurrence. For the
crossed family, with $s\equiv\braket{\alpha,m}{\beta,n}$,
\begin{equation}
\mathcal N^2=1+2\mu\nu s^2,\qquad
p_A=p_B=\frac{\nu^2(1-s^2)}{\mathcal N^2},\qquad
\det\rho=\frac{\mu^2\nu^2(1-s^2)^2}{\mathcal N^4},
\qquad \omega_A=+\Delta,\;\;\omega_B=-\Delta ,
\label{eq:pB-crossed}
\end{equation}
where now $p_A=p_B$ holds \emph{identically}, because the two branches are
exchanged copies of one another and therefore $N_1=N_2$.

\subsection{Exact antisymmetry theorem for the crossed family}
\label{sec:antisymmetry}

Combining $p_B=p_A$ with $\omega_B=-\omega_A$ gives $b_B=-b_A$, and inspecting
Eq.~\eqref{eq:ZU-def} term by term:
$(-1)^{\omega_B}=(-1)^{-\Delta}=(-1)^{\Delta}$ and $\cos(\pi b_B)=\cos(\pi b_A)$,
so $\mathrm{Re}\,Z_U^B=\mathrm{Re}\,Z_U^A$; while $\omega_B/b_B=\omega_A/b_A$ but
$\sin(\pi b_B)=-\sin(\pi b_A)$, so $\mathrm{Im}\,Z_U^B=-\mathrm{Im}\,Z_U^A$.
Therefore $Z_U^B=\overline{Z_U^{A}}$ and
\begin{equation}
\boxed{\;\Phi_U^B=-\,\Phi_U^A,
\qquad\text{i.e.}\qquad \Phi_U^A+\Phi_U^B=0\;}
\label{eq:antisymmetry}
\end{equation}
\emph{exactly}, for every $\alpha,\beta,\mu,m,n$. Under the global cycle the two
holonomies cancel: the entangled pair accumulates no \emph{net} Uhlmann
holonomy even though each subsystem separately accumulates a nonzero one. This
is verified numerically to $|\Phi_U^A+\Phi_U^B|=0$ (exactly zero in floating
point) in Sec.~\ref{sec:numerics}, and is displayed in
Fig.~\ref{fig:fig8}(c).

\subsection{Symmetric family used for the figures}

Throughout the numerical sections we use
$\beta=\alpha\in\mathbb R$, $\mu\in[0,1]$, $\nu=\sqrt{1-\mu^2}$, and
$q\equiv|g_{mn}(\alpha)|^2$, for which
\begin{equation}
\mathcal N^2=1+2\mu\nu q,\qquad
p(\mu,\alpha;m,n)=\frac{(1-\mu^2)(1-q)}{1+2\mu\sqrt{1-\mu^2}\,q},\qquad
C(\mu,\alpha;m,n)=\frac{2\mu\sqrt{1-\mu^2}(1-q)}{1+2\mu\sqrt{1-\mu^2}\,q},
\label{eq:pC-symmetric}
\end{equation}
and $\det\rho_A=(C/2)^2$, so that Eq.~\eqref{eq:phiU-closed} can be
evaluated at any point of the $(\mu,\alpha)$ plane.

\section{Uhlmann nodal singularities}
\label{sec:nodal}
% =========================================================================

Define the complex Uhlmann amplitude
\begin{equation}
Z_U = (-1)^\Delta\cos(\pi b) - i\,\frac{\Delta(1-2p)}{b}\sin(\pi b),
\qquad \Phi_U=\Arg Z_U ,
\label{eq:ZU-def}
\end{equation}
with real and imaginary parts
\begin{equation}
\mathrm{Re}\,Z_U=(-1)^\Delta\cos(\pi b),\qquad
\mathrm{Im}\,Z_U=-(-1)^\Delta\,\frac{\Delta(1-2p)}{b}\sin(\pi b).
\label{eq:ZU-parts}
\end{equation}
A genuine phase singularity requires \emph{both} parts to vanish
simultaneously. Since $\cos(\pi b)=0\Rightarrow\sin(\pi b)\neq0$, this can
only happen if the coefficient of $\sin(\pi b)$ in the imaginary part
\emph{also} vanishes, forcing $1-2p=0$ independently of $b$. The joint
nodal condition is therefore
\begin{equation}
p=\frac12,\qquad b=k+\frac12,\quad k\in\mathbb Z.
\label{eq:nodal-condition}
\end{equation}
At $p=1/2$, Eq.~\eqref{eq:b-at-half} gives $b=\Delta\,C$ (recall
$\omega=\Delta$ here), so $b=k+1/2$ fixes the concurrence to the discrete set
\begin{equation}
C_k = \frac{2k+1}{2\Delta},\qquad k=0,1,\dots,\Delta-1 ,
\label{eq:Ck-def}
\end{equation}
where the upper bound on $k$ enforces the physical requirement $C_k\le1$.
This is a qualitatively different statement from the one reached with the
sandwich connection, which gave $C_k=2\Delta/(2k+1)$ with $k\ge\Delta$, i.e.\
an \emph{infinite} tower accumulating at $C\to0$. With the correct connection
the nodal set is \textbf{finite}: a given photon-addition separation $\Delta$
supports \emph{exactly $\Delta$} Uhlmann singularities, at concurrences
equally spaced by $1/\Delta$ and centred on the interval $(0,1)$. In
Sec.~\ref{sec:numerics} we confirm directly that $|Z_U|$ vanishes to
$\sim10^{-15}$ at each $C_k$ of Eq.~\eqref{eq:Ck-def}, and does \emph{not}
vanish at the values predicted by the earlier formula.

\subsection{Analytic nodal coordinates in the $(\mu,\alpha)$ plane}

For the symmetric family, $p=1/2$ in Eq.~\eqref{eq:pC-symmetric} solves
exactly for $\mu$ in terms of $q$: from
$(1-\mu^2)(1-q)=\tfrac12\big[1+2\mu\sqrt{1-\mu^2}\,q\big]$ one finds, after
simplification, $\mu=\nu(1-2q)$ (valid for $q\le1/2$), i.e.
\begin{equation}
\mu(q)=\frac{1-2q}{\sqrt{1+(1-2q)^2}},\qquad
\nu(q)=\frac{1}{\sqrt{1+(1-2q)^2}},
\label{eq:mu-nu-of-q}
\end{equation}
along which the concurrence reduces to the remarkably simple
\begin{equation}
C = 1-2q.
\label{eq:C-of-q}
\end{equation}
Combining Eqs.~\eqref{eq:Ck-def} and \eqref{eq:C-of-q} gives the nodal
coordinates directly, without ever having to search the full
two-dimensional phase map numerically:
\begin{equation}
q_k = |g_{mn}(\alpha_k)|^2 = \frac12\left(1-\frac{2k+1}{2\Delta}\right),
\qquad
\mu_k = \frac{C_k}{\sqrt{1+C_k^2}},
\qquad k=0,\dots,\Delta-1 ,
\label{eq:nodal-coords}
\end{equation}
and $\alpha_k$ is obtained by numerically solving $|g_{mn}(\alpha_k)|^2=q_k$
(root-finding is legitimate because $|g_{mn}(\alpha)|^2$ increases
monotonically with $\alpha$, verified numerically in
Sec.~\ref{sec:numerics}). Note that Eqs.~\eqref{eq:mu-nu-of-q} and
\eqref{eq:C-of-q} are purely \emph{kinematic} --- they follow from the state,
not from the connection --- and so are unaffected by the correction of
Sec.~\ref{sec:uhlmann}; only the input $C_k$ changes.

\subsection{Critical amplitude: a bound, not an accumulation point}

The $p=1/2$ curve exists only while $q\le1/2$. Define the critical
amplitude $\alpha_g(m,n)$ by
\begin{equation}
|g_{mn}(\alpha_g)|^2 = \frac12 .
\label{eq:alphag-def}
\end{equation}
Because $|g_{mn}(\alpha)|^2$ increases monotonically for the PACS pairs studied
here, the nodal region is exactly $\alpha\le\alpha_g(m,n)$. Since
$C_k\ge 1/(2\Delta)>0$ for every admissible $k$, Eq.~\eqref{eq:nodal-coords}
gives $q_k\le\frac12(1-\frac{1}{2\Delta})<\frac12$, so
\emph{every} node satisfies $\alpha_k<\alpha_g$ strictly:
$\alpha_g$ remains a genuine boundary for the existence of Uhlmann
singularities. What it is \emph{not} --- contrary to the conclusion reached
with the sandwich connection --- is an accumulation point. There are exactly
$\Delta$ nodes, they are isolated and well separated, and none of them
approaches $(\mu,\alpha)=(0,\alpha_g)$; the largest is the $k=0$ node, at the
smallest concurrence $C_0=1/(2\Delta)$.

The dense $2\pi$ phase wrapping visible near the $\mu\to0$ edge in
Fig.~\ref{fig:fig5} is an ordinary branch-cut effect of $\Arg(\cdot)$ and
must \emph{not} be mistaken for physical singularities: the true zeros of
$Z_U$ are exactly the $\Delta$ isolated points given by
Eqs.~\eqref{eq:Ck-def}--\eqref{eq:nodal-coords}, tabulated in
Table~\ref{tab:table2} together with the verified value of $|Z_U|$ at each.

\section{Nodal singularities of subsystem $B$}
\label{sec:nodalB}
% =========================================================================

Having established in Sec.~\ref{sec:subsystemB} that $\rho_B$ carries its own
holonomy, we now derive its nodal structure. The logic is identical to
Sec.~\ref{sec:nodal} but with the quantities belonging to $B$. A genuine zero
of
\begin{equation}
Z_U^B=(-1)^{\omega_B}\cos(\pi b_B)-i\,\frac{\omega_B(1-2p_B)}{b_B}\sin(\pi b_B),
\qquad
b_B=\omega_B\sqrt{1+C^2-4p_B(1-p_B)} ,
\label{eq:ZUB}
\end{equation}
requires both parts to vanish; since $\cos(\pi b_B)=0\Rightarrow\sin(\pi b_B)\neq0$,
the factor $(1-2p_B)$ must vanish independently, giving
\begin{equation}
\boxed{\;p_B=\tfrac12,\qquad |b_B|=k+\tfrac12,\quad k\in\mathbb Z_{\ge0}\;}
\label{eq:nodalB-condition}
\end{equation}

\subsection{The tower of nodal concurrences is the same as for $A$}

At $p_B=1/2$, Eq.~\eqref{eq:b-at-half} gives $|b_B|=|\omega_B|\,C=\Delta\,C$,
so Eq.~\eqref{eq:nodalB-condition} quantizes the concurrence to
\begin{equation}
C_k=\frac{2k+1}{2\Delta},\qquad k=0,1,\dots,\Delta-1
\label{eq:CkB}
\end{equation}
--- \emph{exactly the same finite set} as Eq.~\eqref{eq:Ck-def} for $A$. This holds
in both families, including the crossed one where $\omega_B=-\Delta$, because
only $|\omega_B|$ enters, and because the concurrence is shared between the two
subsystems. The quantized entanglement values at which mixed-state phase
singularities occur are therefore a property of the \emph{pair}, not of either
subsystem separately: each side carries exactly $\Delta$ Uhlmann
singularities, at the same $\Delta$ values of the concurrence.

\subsection{The location of the nodes is \emph{not} the same}

The nodes of $B$ live on the curve $p_B=1/2$, which differs from $p_A=1/2$
whenever $\beta\neq\alpha$. For the branch-correlated family, writing
$G\equiv g_{mn}(\alpha)$ and $H\equiv h_{mn}(\beta)$, Eq.~\eqref{eq:pB-correlated}
gives the implicit condition
\begin{equation}
2(1-\mu^2)(1-H^2)\;=\;1+2\mu\sqrt{1-\mu^2}\;G\,H ,
\label{eq:pB-half-curve}
\end{equation}
solved for $\mu$ at each $\alpha$. Setting $H=G$ reduces
Eq.~\eqref{eq:pB-half-curve} identically to $\mu=\nu(1-2q)$, i.e.\
Eq.~\eqref{eq:mu-nu-of-q}, as it must.

\subsection{Existence criterion: controlled by $\beta$, not by $\alpha$}

Evaluating the difference of the two sides of Eq.~\eqref{eq:pB-half-curve} at
the endpoints gives $1-2H^2$ at $\mu=0$ and $-1$ at $\mu=1$. A root in
$(0,1)$ therefore exists if and only if
\begin{equation}
|h_{mn}(\beta)|^2<\tfrac12
\qquad\Longleftrightarrow\qquad
\beta<\alpha_g(m,n),
\label{eq:B-existence}
\end{equation}
\emph{independently of $\alpha$}. This is a sharp structural contrast with
subsystem $A$, whose criterion is $\alpha\le\alpha_g$ and which therefore
carries a horizontal boundary in the $(\mu,\alpha)$ plane. The geometric
consequence is that the $\Delta$ nodes of $A$ all lie \emph{below} $\alpha_g$
(Sec.~\ref{sec:nodal}), whereas the nodes of $B$ are not bounded by
$\alpha_g$ at all: the curve $p_B=1/2$ is nearly vertical and crosses the
entire range of $\alpha$, so at fixed $\beta<\alpha_g$ the $B$ nodes can sit
far above $\alpha_g$. For $(m,n)=(0,2)$ at $\beta=0.8$, for instance, the
$k=0$ node of $A$ is at $\alpha=1.906<\alpha_g=2.109$ while that of $B$ is at
$\alpha=5.394$, more than twice $\alpha_g$. Both subsystems nonetheless carry
exactly $\Delta$ nodes, at the same $\Delta$ concurrences.

Table~\ref{tab:nodesB} lists the nodes of both subsystems for a
representative case, and Fig.~\ref{fig:fig9} displays the two phase maps side
by side. Every entry was verified to satisfy $p=1/2$, $|b|=k+1/2$ and
$|Z_U|<10^{-13}$ simultaneously.

\subsection{The strictly local cycle: what actually happens to $B$}
\label{sec:soloA}

It is worth stating precisely what the $A$-only cycle
$U=e^{-ia_A^\dagger a_A\phi}\otimes I_B$ does to $B$, since two distinct
objects are easily conflated.

\paragraph{The state of $B$ does not move.}
By Eq.~\eqref{eq:no-signalling}, $\rho_B(\phi)=\rho_B(0)$ exactly, so the path
of $\rho_B$ is a \emph{point}, not a loop. Computing its holonomy with the
polar-decomposition algorithm --- which assumes nothing --- returns
$U_{\rm hol}=\mathbb 1$ and
\begin{equation}
\Phi_U^B=\Arg\Tr[\rho_B\,\mathbb 1]=\Arg(1)=0 ,
\end{equation}
verified numerically as $|\Tr[\rho_B U_{\rm hol}]|=1.000000000000$ and
$\Phi_U^B\sim4\times10^{-19}$. This is unavoidable: Uhlmann holonomy is
holonomy \emph{along a path in the base}, and here the base path is constant.

\paragraph{The purification amplitude of $B$ does move.}
Write the global state as $\ket{\Phi}=\sum_{jk}C_{jk}\ket{j}_A\ket{k}_B$. The
coefficient matrix is literally an amplitude for $\rho_B$: setting
$W_B=C^{T}$ gives $W_BW_B^\dagger=C^TC^*=\rho_B$. Under the local cycle
$C\mapsto U_AC$, so
\begin{equation}
W_B(\phi)=W_B(0)\,U_A^{T}(\phi) .
\label{eq:WB-motion}
\end{equation}
$\rho_B$ is invariant because $U_A^T$ is unitary, but the amplitude traverses a
\emph{vertical} loop in the fibre over the fixed point $\rho_B$. Its overlap
with the initial amplitude is, exactly,
\begin{equation}
\boxed{\;\Tr\!\big[W_B^\dagger(0)\,W_B(\phi)\big]
=\Tr\!\big[\rho_A(0)\,U_A(\phi)\big]\;}
\label{eq:fibre-overlap}
\end{equation}
which follows from $W_B^\dagger(0)W_B(\phi)=C^*C^TU_A^T$ together with
$C^*C^T=\rho_A^*$ and $\Tr[\rho_A^*U_A^T]=\Tr[U_A\rho_A]$; we verify
Eq.~\eqref{eq:fibre-overlap} numerically to $1.6\times10^{-16}$. Thus
\emph{the motion of $B$'s fibre is governed entirely by the reduced state of
$A$}. Note the formal parallel with $\Phi_U=\Arg\Tr[\rho_A(0)U_{\rm hol}]$:
here the \emph{physical} unitary appears in place of the holonomy.

This Pancharatnam-type phase is nonzero throughout the cycle and closes at
$\phi=2\pi$, because $a^\dagger a$ has integer spectrum and
$U_A(2\pi)=\mathbb 1$ exactly. Within the logical subspace, if one adopts the
convention of retaining only the $\sigma_z$ part of the generator (discarding
the scalar factor $e^{-i(m+n)\phi/2}$), the amplitude of $B$ returns multiplied
by $V^T(2\pi)=(-1)^{\omega}$ --- a $\mathbb Z_2$ residue equal to $-1$ for odd
$\Delta$, and the same $(-1)^\omega$ that prefixes $Z_U$.

\paragraph{Summary.} Under a strictly local $A$-cycle the Uhlmann phase of $B$
is zero, and this is a theorem rather than a modelling choice. Entanglement
manifests itself on the $B$ side not as motion of the state but as motion of
its purification. A nonzero Uhlmann holonomy for $B$ requires a cycle that
actually moves $\rho_B$: either $B$'s own cycle or the global two-mode
evolution of Eq.~\eqref{eq:global-cycle}.

\subsection{Nodal set of the crossed family}

In the crossed family $p_A=p_B$ identically and $\omega_B=-\omega_A$, so
$Z_U^B=\overline{Z_U^A}$ by Eq.~\eqref{eq:antisymmetry}. Complex conjugation
does not move zeros, hence the two subsystems share \emph{exactly the same
nodal set} in parameter space, while their phase maps are mirror images. The
crossed family is thus the extreme opposite of the asymmetric
branch-correlated case: identical singularities, opposite holonomies.

\section{Numerical validation methodology}
\label{sec:numerics}
% =========================================================================

A central addition of this expanded treatment, relative to the original
manuscript, is a systematic \emph{independent} numerical validation layer,
implemented in the accompanying Jupyter notebook
(\texttt{pacs\_berry\_uhlmann.ipynb}) and summarized here.

\subsection{Truncated Fock-space ground truth}

Every PACS branch $\ket{\alpha,n}$ is expanded directly from
Eq.~\eqref{eq:pacs-fock} with a numerically large cutoff $N_{\rm cut}$
(typically 400--500 Fock levels, verified to be more than sufficient for
the coherent amplitudes $\alpha\lesssim3$ used throughout). Overlaps
$\braket{\alpha,m}{\alpha,n}$ and number-operator matrix elements
$\bra{\alpha,m}a^\dagger a\ket{\alpha,n}$ are then obtained by direct
term-by-term summation, with \emph{no} reference to confluent
hypergeometric functions or Laguerre-polynomial recursions beyond the
normalization itself. This provides a completely independent numerical
benchmark for every closed-form expression derived above.

\subsection{Correcting a prefactor ambiguity in Eqs.~(27) and (37)}

Repeating the derivation of $g_{mn}(\alpha)$ from Eq.~\eqref{eq:pacs-fock}
in full (Appendix~\ref{app:overlaps}) shows that the factor $(n-m)!\equiv\Delta!$
must appear \emph{outside} the square root in the denominator of the
prefactor, i.e.\ Eq.~\eqref{eq:g-overlap-corrected} above, rather than as
$\sqrt{\Delta!}$ as printed in the original manuscript. Numerically, the
two conventions differ by a factor $\sqrt{\Delta!}$ whenever $\Delta>1$
(they coincide for $\Delta=1$, which is presumably why the discrepancy was
not caught against low-order checks). We verified Eq.~\eqref{eq:g-overlap-corrected}
in two independent ways: (a) direct comparison against the truncated
Fock-space overlap, agreeing to machine precision ($<10^{-14}$) for all
tested $(\alpha,m,n)$; and (b) root-finding the critical amplitudes
$\alpha_g(m,n)$ from Eq.~\eqref{eq:alphag-def} and comparing against every
entry of the original manuscript's Table~I (reproduced here as
Table~\ref{tab:table1}), with agreement to $4\times10^{-5}$, consistent
with the four decimal digits originally reported. The corrected prefactor
was then propagated into the cross-number-operator element
$\kappa_\alpha^{mn}$ (Appendix~\ref{app:cross-element}); note that the
\emph{dimensionless ratio} $Q_{mn}=\kappa_\alpha^{mn}/g_{mn}$,
Eq.~\eqref{eq:Qmn-def}, is insensitive to this prefactor ambiguity because
it cancels in the ratio, which is why Eqs.~(38)--(39) of the original
manuscript required no correction.

\section{Discussion}
% =========================================================================

The model developed here separates cleanly into three geometric levels of
increasing structural complexity.

\emph{Level 1 --- single-branch Berry phase.} A single PACS under a number
rotation probes only the mean photon number, Eq.~\eqref{eq:berry-single}: a
smooth, single-valued, monotonic-in-$\alpha$ quantity with no possibility
of singular behavior, because it is a pure-state Berry phase of a
one-dimensional cyclic evolution.

\emph{Level 2 --- global bipartite Berry phase.} Entangling two PACS
branches adds an \emph{interference correction}, $g^2Q_{mn}$ in
Eq.~\eqref{eq:berry-vs-C}, that carries information about the branch
overlap and, in the balanced case, is an \emph{explicit affine function of
the concurrence}. This is still a pure-state Berry phase (of the global
state $\ket\Phi$) and therefore remains smooth everywhere, as confirmed by
the featureless map of Fig.~\ref{fig:fig4}.

\emph{Level 3 --- reduced-state Uhlmann holonomy.} Once one traces out
subsystem $B$, the reduced state $\rho_A$ is genuinely mixed, and its
holonomy is no longer guaranteed to be smooth: Eq.~\eqref{eq:phiU-closed}
develops \emph{exact} phase singularities whenever
Eq.~\eqref{eq:nodal-condition} holds. Crucially, the Uhlmann phase cannot
in general be written as a function of concurrence alone (unlike the
Level-2 Berry phase), because the population $p$ is an \emph{independent}
geometric variable; only exactly at the nodal points does $p=1/2$ remove
that freedom and lock the singular concurrence to the quantized values
$C_k$ of Eq.~\eqref{eq:Ck-def}.

The critical function $\alpha_g(m,n)$ is a genuinely different kind of
object from a thermodynamic critical point: it is fixed \emph{entirely} by
the overlap of the two local PACS branches (Eq.~\eqref{eq:alphag-def}), and
it separates a region of coherent amplitude where equal logical-basis
population ($p=1/2$) is kinematically accessible from a region where it is
not. Physically, larger $\alpha$ makes the two photon-added branches
$\ket{\alpha,m}$ and $\ket{\alpha,n}$ increasingly \emph{distinguishable}
(their Fock-space overlap $|g_{mn}(\alpha)|^2$ approaches~1, cf.\
Fig.~\ref{fig:fig6}a), which pushes the achievable population imbalance
away from the symmetric point required for an exact node.

\emph{Level 3 has two sides.} The most consequential revision in this edition
is the recognition that Level 3 is not a statement about $A$ alone. Tracing out
either subsystem of the same entangled pure state leaves a mixed state, and by
Schmidt isospectrality the two carry \emph{the same} concurrence and hence the
same $\det\rho=(C/2)^2$. What distinguishes them is not their entanglement but
their kinematics: the winding number $\omega$ and the population $p$. The
earlier claim that $B$ has ``zero geometric phase'' conflated a property of one
chosen cycle --- a strictly local rotation of mode $A$, under which
Eq.~\eqref{eq:no-signalling} forbids any change in $\rho_B$ --- with a property
of the state. Under the free two-mode evolution
$e^{-i(a_A^\dagger a_A+a_B^\dagger a_B)\phi}$, which is what an actual
propagating entangled field undergoes, both reduced states cycle and both
holonomies are nonzero.

Two consequences deserve emphasis. First, the crossed family exhibits an exact
cancellation $\Phi_U^A+\Phi_U^B=0$, Eq.~\eqref{eq:antisymmetry}: the pair
accumulates no net mixed-state holonomy even though each half accumulates a
large one, a purely kinematic effect of the interchanged photon-addition
indices ($\omega_B=-\omega_A$). Second, and more surprising, the shared
concurrence does \emph{not} imply a shared singularity structure. The tower of
nodal concurrences $C_k=2\Delta/(2k+1)$ is common to both sides --- it is a
property of the entangled pair --- yet the nodes sit at different points of
parameter space and, in the asymmetric branch-correlated family, are governed
by different existence criteria: $A$ requires $\alpha\le\alpha_g$, which bounds
all $\Delta$ of its nodes from above, whereas $B$ requires $\beta<\alpha_g$, a
condition on the \emph{other} amplitude that leaves its nodes free to sit well
beyond $\alpha_g$ (Fig.~\ref{fig:fig9}). Entanglement fixes \emph{which} concurrences are
singular; the local overlap geometry of each mode fixes \emph{where} those
singularities are realized.

\section{Conclusions}
% =========================================================================

We have presented a unified, extensively re-derived and numerically
cross-validated Berry--Uhlmann treatment of entangled photon-added coherent
states. Relative to the original manuscript, this expanded edition:
\begin{enumerate}[label=(\roman*)]
\item spells out every intermediate algebraic step from the PACS Fock
      expansion to the final closed forms (Secs.~\ref{sec:pacs}--\ref{sec:nodal},
      Appendices~\ref{app:overlaps}--\ref{app:cross-element});
\item generalizes the global Berry phase to arbitrary branch weight $\mu$
      and exhibits it as a genuine two-dimensional phase map
      (Eq.~\eqref{eq:general-berry-formula}, Fig.~\ref{fig:fig4});
\item identifies and corrects a prefactor transcription ambiguity in the
      overlap and cross-number-operator formulas, verified to machine
      precision against an independent Fock-space calculation and against
      both published numerical tables (Sec.~\ref{sec:numerics});
\item \textbf{corrects the treatment of subsystem $B$}, which earlier
      presentations dismissed as carrying zero geometric phase. That
      statement holds only for a strictly local cycle on $A$, where
      no-signalling forbids any change in $\rho_B$
      (Eq.~\eqref{eq:no-signalling}); because $\rho_B$ descends from an
      entangled PACS it is isospectral to $\rho_A$, hence mixed with
      $\det\rho_B=(C/2)^2>0$, and carries a genuine holonomy under its own
      cycle or under the global two-mode evolution
      (Secs.~\ref{sec:subsystemB}--\ref{sec:antisymmetry},
      Fig.~\ref{fig:fig8});
\item derives the complete nodal structure of $B$ (Sec.~\ref{sec:nodalB},
      Fig.~\ref{fig:fig9}): the same tower $C_k=2\Delta/(2k+1)$ as for $A$,
      but at different locations and with an existence criterion
      $\beta<\alpha_g(m,n)$ that is independent of $\alpha$, so that the
      nodes of $A$ are bounded by $\alpha_g$ while those of $B$ are not;
      and proves the exact antisymmetry $\Phi_U^B=-\Phi_U^A$ of the
      crossed family;
\item packages every calculation into a fully commented, openly
      reproducible Python/Jupyter implementation that regenerates all
      nine figures and every table and exports them as
      publication-quality vector PDFs.
\end{enumerate}
The central nodal result, now derived from the corrected Uhlmann connection,
independently verified against two connection-free numerical constructions of
the holonomy, and --- by Sec.~\ref{sec:nodalB} --- valid for \emph{both}
subsystems with $\Delta\to|\omega_s|$ and $p\to p_s$, is
\begin{equation}
p=\frac12,\qquad
C_k = \frac{2k+1}{2(n-m)},\qquad
|g_{mn}(\alpha_k)|^2 = \frac{1-C_k}{2},
\qquad k=0,\dots,\Delta-1 ,
\label{eq:central-result}
\end{equation}
so that each photon-addition separation supports exactly $\Delta$ isolated
Uhlmann singularities, all strictly below the critical amplitude $\alpha_g$
defined by $|g_{mn}(\alpha_g)|^2=1/2$ but not accumulating at it. This provides a direct, fully
analytic bridge between photon addition, nonorthogonality, bipartite
entanglement, and mixed-state quantum holonomy, and establishes PACS as an
unusually transparent laboratory in which every step of that bridge can be
written --- and independently checked --- in closed form. The two-sided
analysis adds a further lesson: entanglement, through the shared concurrence,
dictates \emph{which} values are geometrically singular, while the local
overlap geometry of each mode dictates \emph{where} in parameter space those
singularities actually occur.

\appendix
\section{Scalar products of photon-added coherent states}
\label{app:overlaps}
% =========================================================================

We give here the full derivation of Eq.~\eqref{eq:g-overlap-corrected},
including the step that fixes the prefactor unambiguously (this is the
step whose printed form differs between this expanded edition and the
original manuscript).

Insert the Fock expansion Eq.~\eqref{eq:pacs-fock} of each bra and ket and
use Fock-state orthogonality: for $0\le m\le n$, the surviving terms are
those with equal absolute Fock level $\ell=m+j=n+k$ ($j,k\ge0$), i.e.\
$j=\ell-m$, $k=\ell-n$, valid for $\ell\ge n$ (automatically $j\ge0$ since
$n\ge m$). Writing $A_n\equiv e^{-|\alpha|^2/2}/\sqrt{n!\,L_n(-|\alpha|^2)}$
for the normalization constant of Eq.~\eqref{eq:pacs-fock},
\begin{equation}
\braket{\alpha,m}{\beta,n} = A_mA_n\sum_{\ell=n}^{\infty}
\frac{(\alpha^*)^{\ell-m}\sqrt{\ell!}}{(\ell-m)!}\cdot
\frac{\beta^{\ell-n}\sqrt{\ell!}}{(\ell-n)!}.
\label{eq:overlap-sum-raw}
\end{equation}
Setting $\ell=n+p$ ($p=0,1,2,\dots$) and $\Delta\equiv n-m$,
\begin{equation}
\braket{\alpha,m}{\beta,n} = A_mA_n\,(\alpha^*)^{\Delta}
\sum_{p=0}^{\infty}(\alpha^*\beta)^{p}\,\frac{(n+p)!}{(\Delta+p)!\,p!}.
\label{eq:overlap-sum-p}
\end{equation}
The key algebraic step, made fully explicit here, is to recognize the
ratio of factorials as a ratio of Pochhammer (rising-factorial) symbols
evaluated at the summation index $p$:
\begin{equation}
\frac{(n+p)!}{(\Delta+p)!} = \frac{n!}{\Delta!}\cdot\frac{(n+1)_p}{(\Delta+1)_p},
\qquad (a)_p \equiv a(a+1)\cdots(a+p-1) = \frac{\Gamma(a+p)}{\Gamma(a)}.
\label{eq:pochhammer-identity}
\end{equation}
Substituting Eq.~\eqref{eq:pochhammer-identity} into
Eq.~\eqref{eq:overlap-sum-p} converts the sum directly into the confluent
hypergeometric series,
\begin{equation}
\sum_{p=0}^{\infty}\frac{(n+1)_p}{(\Delta+1)_p}\,\frac{z^p}{p!} = \hyp{n+1}{\Delta+1}{z},
\qquad z\equiv\alpha^*\beta ,
\label{eq:1F1-series}
\end{equation}
so that, using $A_mA_n=e^{-\frac12(|\alpha|^2+|\beta|^2)}/\sqrt{m!\,n!\,L_m(-|\alpha|^2)L_n(-|\beta|^2)}$,
\begin{equation}
\boxed{\;
\braket{\alpha,m}{\beta,n} =
\frac{e^{-\frac12(|\alpha|^2+|\beta|^2)}(\alpha^*)^{\Delta}\,n!}
{\Delta!\;\sqrt{m!\,n!\,L_m(-|\alpha|^2)L_n(-|\beta|^2)}}\;
\hyp{n+1}{\Delta+1}{\alpha^*\beta}
\;}
\label{eq:overlap-general-corrected}
\end{equation}
for $0\le m\le n$. This is the general (complex-amplitude) form of
Eq.~\eqref{eq:g-overlap-corrected}, with the crucial factor $n!/\Delta!$
appearing as a single ratio \emph{outside} the square root --- as
Eq.~\eqref{eq:pochhammer-identity} makes explicit, this ratio arises
naturally from the Pochhammer-symbol reduction and has no reason to be
split across the square root. Setting $\beta=\alpha$ real recovers
Eq.~\eqref{eq:g-overlap-corrected}. We verified
Eq.~\eqref{eq:overlap-general-corrected} against the direct truncated
Fock-space sum, Eq.~\eqref{eq:overlap-sum-raw} evaluated numerically term
by term, for dozens of $(\alpha,m,n)$ combinations with $\Delta$ ranging
from 1 to 4, finding agreement to better than $10^{-14}$ in every case
(machine precision), and further confirmed indirectly through exact
reproduction of the published Table~I (Sec.~\ref{sec:numerics}).

\section{Derivation of the cross number matrix element}
\label{app:cross-element}
% =========================================================================

For real $\alpha$ and $n\ge m$, we need
$\kappa_\alpha^{mn}\equiv\bra{\alpha,m}a^\dagger a\ket{\alpha,n}$. Repeating
the sum of Appendix~\ref{app:overlaps} but weighting each term by its Fock
eigenvalue $\ell=n+p$ of $a^\dagger a$,
\begin{equation}
\kappa_\alpha^{mn} = A_mA_n\,\alpha^\Delta\sum_{p=0}^{\infty}(n+p)\,\alpha^{2p}\,
\frac{(n+p)!}{(\Delta+p)!\,p!}
= A_mA_n\,\alpha^\Delta\Big[n\,S(x)+x\,S'(x)\Big],
\qquad x\equiv\alpha^2,
\label{eq:kappa-sum}
\end{equation}
where $S(x)\equiv\sum_p x^p(n+p)!/[(\Delta+p)!p!] = (n!/\Delta!)\hyp{n+1}{\Delta+1}{x}$
is exactly the sum appearing in $g_{mn}$ (Appendix~\ref{app:overlaps}), and
the second equality follows from
$\sum_p p\,x^pf_p = x\,\frac{d}{dx}\sum_p x^pf_p$. Using the standard
derivative identity for the confluent hypergeometric function,
$\frac{d}{dx}\hyp{a}{b}{x}=\frac{a}{b}\hyp{a+1}{b+1}{x}$, and a Kummer
contiguous relation connecting $\hyp{n+2}{\Delta+2}{x}$ to
$\hyp{n+2}{\Delta+1}{x}$ and $\hyp{n+1}{\Delta+1}{x}$, the bracket in
Eq.~\eqref{eq:kappa-sum} reduces --- after some algebra, and verified
independently by direct numerical differentiation of $S(x)$ against the
brute-force Fock sum --- to the compact closed form
\begin{equation}
\kappa_\alpha^{mn} = g_{mn}(\alpha)\,Q_{mn}(x),
\qquad
Q_{mn}(x) = (n+1)\,\frac{\hyp{n+2}{\Delta+1}{x}}{\hyp{n+1}{\Delta+1}{x}} - 1 .
\label{eq:Qmn-def}
\end{equation}
Equation~\eqref{eq:Qmn-def} is a \emph{dimensionless ratio}
$\kappa_\alpha^{mn}/g_{mn}(\alpha)$, so the prefactor ambiguity discussed
in Appendix~\ref{app:overlaps} cancels identically and does not affect this
formula; it therefore agrees with the original manuscript's Eqs.~(38)--(39)
without modification. We verified Eq.~\eqref{eq:Qmn-def} against the
truncated Fock-space sum $\bra{\alpha,m}a^\dagger a\ket{\alpha,n}$
(Sec.~\ref{sec:numerics}) for the same set of $(\alpha,m,n)$ used in
Appendix~\ref{app:overlaps}, with agreement better than $10^{-7}$.

\section{Numerical conventions and reproducibility}
% =========================================================================

All numerical results use real coherent amplitudes and the symmetric
family $\beta=\alpha$ unless otherwise stated. Balanced plots take
$\mu=\nu=1/\sqrt2$; two-dimensional weight maps impose
$\nu=\sqrt{1-\mu^2}$ with $0\le\mu\le1$. Uhlmann-phase plots display the
principal value $\Arg Z_U\in(-\pi,\pi]$. The gray region above $\alpha_g$
in Fig.~\ref{fig:fig5} is not a numerical cutoff: it is the analytic
region in which $p=1/2$ has no solution for the symmetric model,
Eq.~\eqref{eq:mu-nu-of-q}. Root-finding for $\alpha_g(m,n)$ and for
$\alpha_k$ uses bisection (\texttt{scipy.optimize.brentq}) on the
monotonic function $\alpha\mapsto|g_{mn}(\alpha)|^2$; Laguerre polynomials
and the confluent hypergeometric function are evaluated with
\texttt{scipy.special.eval\_genlaguerre} and \texttt{scipy.special.hyp1f1}
respectively. The complete, independently executable code --- including
every closed-form routine, the truncated-Fock-space validation layer, and
the figure-generation routines with on-screen display and vector-PDF export
--- accompanies this manuscript as the Jupyter notebook
\texttt{pacs\_berry\_uhlmann.ipynb}.
